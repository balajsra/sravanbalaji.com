I was a big fan of [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/) for many years. Their pricing was reasonable considering you get unlimited access to the world's largest catalog of music. As with most other streaming services though, it got worse over time. They started introducing features I didn't necessarily want (e.g., audiobooks) and increasing their prices. To their credit, they did eventually introduce [lossless audio quality](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/lossless-audio-quality/). However, my philosophy towards media had changed. Streaming is relatively affordable and convenient, but I found myself only listening to music recommended by Spotify's algorithms. I wanted to explore things outside the mainstream and actually *own* the music I listen to. Not just rent them for eternity.

I had already started going down the [Self Hosting](https://github.com/awesome-selfhosted/awesome-selfhosted) rabbit hole, so setting up a music server seemed like a natural next step. [Navidrome](https://www.navidrome.org/) is perfect for this. It allows you to stream DRM-free music you own from your own server to your devices. It works very similarly to Spotify, except you own the files. Purchasing DRM-free music from sites like [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com) and [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/) has the benefit of supporting artists directly, but it is definitely not cheap. However, this helped me be more purposeful in the kinds of music I listen to. I find myself listening to full length albums a lot more often than I did when I used spotify.

It isn't for everybody, but if you're interested in replicating the convenience of streaming with music you own, give [Navidrome](https://www.navidrome.org/) a shot.

There are amazing clients like [Feishin](https://feishin.net/) for desktop and [Symfonium](https://www.symfonium.app/) for Android that support Navidrome and give you an experience very similar to Spotify.
